About this page

Hello. This is a simple health education page. It is about learning what happens in pregnancy and how to get ready for the birth.

It is for people who are pregnant, and for partners or family who want to learn as well.

This page is especially for immigrants and ethnic minorities who do not know how the health system works. We will explain things in a simple way, like who to see, where to go, and what to ask.

What you can learn here

  • How to look after yourself in pregnancy
  • What to expect as the weeks go on
  • Signs that mean you should get help
  • What to pack and who to call when labour starts

Who to see and where to go

In the UK you usually see a midwife. If you do not know how to book, ask a GP. You can ask for an interpreter. A partner or family member can come too, if you want them there.

Signs that mean you should get help

Call for help if there is bleeding, the baby is moving less, a bad headache, or the waters break. Do not wait. If it feels like an emergency, call 999.

What to pack for the birth

Pack notes from your appointments, any medicine, comfortable clothes, a phone charger, snacks, and the phone number of the person you will call for support.

Coping with labour pain

Important: These are simple ideas only. They are not medical advice. If you are worried, call your midwife or 999.
  • Breathe slowly in through your nose and out through your mouth
  • Change position, for example stand, sit on a chair, or lean forward
  • Ask someone to rub your back or hold your hand
  • Focus on one thing for a short time, like a message or a picture
